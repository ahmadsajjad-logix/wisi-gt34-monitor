2. What Is a Frequency, Carrier, Multiplex and Channel?

Frequency / Carrier

The satellite RF signal tuned by the IRD, identified by frequency, polarization and symbol rate.

Multiplex

A single digital transport stream carried on one satellite carrier. One multiplex can contain many television, radio or data services.

DVB Service

An individual service identified by a Service ID (SID), such as a TV channel, radio service, regional feed or data service.

Elementary Stream

The actual video, audio or other encoded component carried inside a DVB service, identified through stream/PID information.

6. What Services Are Being Received?

TV / Video Services

All 43 production carrier inputs were proven TV-bearing at carrier level through explicit elementary-stream evidence.

Radio / Audio Services

Some multiplexes also contain radio/audio-only services. These remain part of service-integrity monitoring even though they do not need video.

Data / Unknown

A service may be present without recognized video or audio elementary-stream metadata. Such cases are reported as metadata/integrity conditions, not automatically as black video.

Unavailable Expected Services

If an expected SID disappears, the monitoring system treats the missing service as a service-integrity fault.

Current final scope: 168 active DVB service instances. A refreshed V6-wide classification is required before publishing a new definitive breakdown of unique TV vs radio vs data service names. 7. Core Parameters Monitored — Explained Simply

1. Carrier / TS Health
Overall answer: is the satellite signal and its digital transport stream usable?
2. Demod Lock
Whether the receiver has successfully locked onto the satellite signal.
3. Transport Stream Present
Whether the digital TV data stream is actually arriving after RF lock.
4. Video Present
Whether recognized video stream information exists for the monitored TV-bearing carrier.
5. Service Integrity Health
Whether the expected TV/radio services are still present and technically identifiable.
6. RF Level
Strength of the received satellite signal.
7. SNR
Signal-to-noise quality: how clearly the useful signal stands above noise.
8. BER
Bit Error Rate: how many digital transmission errors are being detected.
9. Frequency
The satellite RF frequency being monitored.
10. Symbol Rate
The signaling rate used by the digital satellite carrier.
11. TS Bitrate
How much transport-stream data is being received per second.
12. TSID
Transport Stream ID: identifies the multiplex.
13. NID
Network ID: identifies the broadcasting network context.
14. ONID
Original Network ID: identifies the original DVB network.
15. Missing Services
Expected channels/services that are no longer present.
16. Unexpected Services
New or unplanned services appearing in a multiplex.
17. ES Metadata Unavailable
A service is present, but its elementary-stream details cannot currently be confirmed.

8. Additional Technical Information Monitored

The 44-channel PRTG limit does not mean only 44 facts are collected.

Shown as PRTG channels where selected

  • Critical RF and transport health
  • Service-integrity health
  • Per-service health channels where historically registered
  • Selected counts and identifiers

Retained in rich status / database

  • Configured/detected modulation and FEC
  • ISI / MIS and PLS where returned
  • LNB / IF details where returned
  • Network and provider information
  • Full expected/current/running service detail
  • Elementary-stream counts and PID/stream details
  • Missing, ES-N/A and unexpected-service detail
Design principle: PRTG channels are the bounded visual/alarm surface; rich status text provides immediate engineering detail; SQLite preserves the full observation for 15 days.

10. What the Monitoring System Can Detect

Carrier Failure

Loss of demodulator lock on the satellite carrier.

Transport Failure

Carrier remains locked but the MPEG/DVB transport stream disappears.

Service Failure

An expected SID disappears or its elementary-stream metadata becomes unavailable.

RF Degradation

Changes in RF level, SNR or BER can be tracked where returned by the IRD.

Configuration Change

Frequency, symbol rate, service counts, IDs and other transport/service attributes can be observed.

Unexpected Service

New/unplanned SIDs appearing within a monitored multiplex are reported.

Operational detection scope10

11. What the System Does Not Claim

No automatic decoded-picture judgment

ES metadata unavailability is not the same as proving that a TV picture is black or visually off-air.

No unsupported parameter fabrication

If the WISI interface does not expose a value reliably, the system does not invent it.

No name-based TV/radio classification

Service type is determined from stream evidence rather than from the channel name.

No assumption that every service carries video

Radio/audio-only services can be legitimate and still pass service-integrity monitoring.
